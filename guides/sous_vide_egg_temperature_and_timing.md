Egg whites and yolks set at different temperatures. The tight white begins to
set before the loose white, while the yolk changes from liquid to custardy,
fudge-like, and finally firm. Temperature determines the general texture; time
fine-tunes it, especially the yolk.

The observations below are adapted from
[Serious Eats' guide to sous vide eggs](https://www.seriouseats.com/sous-vide-101-all-about-eggs),
which tested large eggs in their shells.

## Temperature guide

These results are for large eggs cooked for $time{40%minutes}. Eggs of a
different size or starting temperature may need a little more or less time to
heat through.

| Bath temperature | Loose white | Tight white | Yolk | Best suited for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $temp{130%F} | Raw and watery | Raw | Raw | Holding or treating eggs for raw preparations; not a ready-to-eat cooked texture |
| $temp{135%F} | Cloudy but raw in texture | Cloudy but raw in texture | Raw | Very lightly warmed eggs |
| $temp{140%F} | Watery | Barely set and delicate | Liquid | Very soft onsen-style eggs |
| $temp{145%F} | Watery and easy to drain away | Tender and spoonable | Liquid to slightly thickened | Poached-style or soft-cooked eggs |
| $temp{150%F} | Watery with some coagulated white | Opaque and firm | Tender, malleable, and shape-holding | Custardy yolks |
| $temp{155%F} | Watery with coagulated white | Firm but tender | Fudge-like | Firm, rich yolks |
| $temp{160%F} | Fully set but tender | Firm but tender | Firm and moist | Fully set whites with a tender yolk |
| $temp{165%F} | Firm but tender | Firm but tender | Firm, moist, and crumbly | Tender hard-cooked eggs and egg salad |

## Time matters at 145°F

At $temp{145%F}, the whites change relatively little after they set, but the
yolk continues to thicken.

| Time | Result |
|---|---|
| $time{45%minutes} | Barely set white and a liquid yolk; a good starting point for a poached-style egg |
| $time{1%hour} | Slightly thicker yolk that briefly holds soft peaks |
| $time{1 1/2%hours} | Yolk as thick as a tender pudding |
| $time{2%hours} | Thick, shape-holding yolk with a soft, gloppy texture |

## Practical targets

| Style | Temperature and time | How to serve |
|---|---|---|
| Poached-style | $temp{143-145%F} for $time{45%minutes} | Crack into a bowl and gently drain away the loose white. For a more traditionally set exterior, briefly lower the egg into barely simmering water. |
| Fast onsen-style | $temp{167%F} for $time{13%minutes} | Serve promptly. The higher temperature firms the white while the yolk only begins to thicken, so accurate timing matters. |
| Hard-cooked but tender | $temp{165%F} for $time{40%minutes} | Chill in an ice bath before peeling. |

## Handling notes

- Lower eggs into the bath gently with a spoon or spider to avoid cracking the shells.
- If serving later, chill cooked eggs promptly in an ice bath and refrigerate them. Rewarm them in $temp{130-140%F} water for about $time{10%minutes}.
- Low-temperature cooking is not automatically pasteurization. Safety depends on both time and temperature. Use pasteurized eggs for vulnerable diners or when a preparation will remain raw or undercooked, and follow current food safety guidance.
