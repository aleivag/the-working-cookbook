Smoke point is the temperature at which a fat begins to produce continuous
visible smoke. These values are approximate: cultivar, refinement, age, reuse,
and manufacturing method can change an oil's actual smoke point. Check the
manufacturer's label when precise temperature tolerance matters.

| Oil or fat | Approximate smoke point | Common uses | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Refined avocado oil | $temp{500-520%F} | High-heat searing, grilling, frying | Neutral flavor and one of the highest common smoke points |
| Refined safflower oil | $temp{450-510%F} | Searing, deep-frying, roasting | Mild flavor; smoke point varies substantially by processing |
| Ghee or clarified butter | $temp{450-485%F} | Sautéing, searing, frying | Milk solids have been removed |
| Refined olive oil | $temp{465%F} | Sautéing, roasting, shallow-frying | Milder flavor than extra-virgin olive oil |
| Refined peanut oil | $temp{450%F} | Deep-frying, wok cooking, searing | Mild nutty flavor; consider peanut allergies |
| Refined soybean oil | $temp{450%F} | Frying, baking, general cooking | Common component of oils labeled “vegetable oil” |
| Refined corn oil | $temp{450%F} | Frying, roasting, baking | Neutral to slightly corn-like flavor |
| Refined sunflower oil | $temp{440-450%F} | Frying, roasting, sautéing | High-oleic versions are especially useful for high heat |
| Grapeseed oil | $temp{420%F} | Sautéing, roasting, dressings | Light flavor; useful when olive-oil flavor is unwanted |
| Canola oil | $temp{400-425%F} | General cooking, baking, frying | Neutral, inexpensive, and versatile |
| Refined coconut oil | $temp{400%F} | Sautéing, baking, medium-high heat | Less coconut flavor than virgin coconut oil |
| Refined sesame oil | $temp{410%F} | Stir-frying and sautéing | Different from strongly flavored toasted sesame oil |
| Extra-virgin olive oil | $temp{350-410%F} | Sautéing, roasting, dressings, finishing | Quality and free-fatty-acid content cause a wide range |
| Virgin coconut oil | $temp{350%F} | Baking and medium-heat cooking | Distinct coconut flavor |
| Toasted sesame oil | $temp{350%F} | Finishing, sauces, marinades | Usually used for flavor rather than as the primary frying oil |
| Butter | $temp{300-350%F} | Low- to medium-heat sautéing, baking | Milk solids brown and burn before the butterfat does |
| Unrefined walnut oil | $temp{320%F} | Dressings, finishing, low-heat cooking | Delicate nut flavor; refrigerate after opening |
| Flaxseed oil | $temp{225%F} | Dressings and finishing only | Highly heat-sensitive; keep refrigerated and do not fry with it |

## Choosing an oil

- For deep-frying or hard searing, choose a refined oil with a smoke point well
  above the intended cooking temperature.
- For ordinary sautéing and roasting, extra-virgin olive oil, canola oil, or
  grapeseed oil usually provides sufficient heat tolerance.
- Use toasted sesame, walnut, and flaxseed oils primarily for flavor and
  finishing.
- Smoke point is not the only measure of performance. Flavor, oxidative
  stability, refinement, and how long the oil remains hot also matter.

Discard oil that smells rancid or produces persistent smoke at the intended
cooking temperature. Avoid repeatedly heating an oil past its smoke point.
