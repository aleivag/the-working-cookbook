La Ratte fingerling potatoes are Keller's preferred variety because their dry,
fine-textured flesh absorbs a large amount of fat. Yukon Gold potatoes are the
more readily available substitute used here. Cook the potatoes whole and
unpeeled so they absorb as little water as possible.

== step heat cream ==
Heat @heavy cream{190%g} until hot but not boiling, then keep it warm to produce
=>hot cream{}.

== step cook potatoes ==
Place @Yukon Gold potatoes{750%g}(whole and unpeeled) in a 3-quart #saucepot{}.
Cover them by 2 inches with @cold water{} and slowly bring the water to a gentle
simmer.

Cook until the potatoes are extremely tender when tested with a #paring knife{},
then turn off the heat to produce =>cooked potatoes{}.

== step pass potatoes ==
Remove one potato at a time from ^cooked potatoes{} with a #slotted spoon{},
split it in half, and press the flesh through the finest screen of a #tamis{}
using a stiff #bowl scraper{}. Leave the skins behind.

Place #parchment paper{} beneath the tamis to collect and produce
=>potato pulp{}.

== step emulsify ==
Transfer ^potato pulp{} to a 4-quart #saucepan{} over medium-low heat. Using a
stiff #rubber spatula{}, incorporate about one-third of ^hot cream{}, then beat
in a few cubes of @unsalted butter{225%g}(cold and cubed) until fully emulsified.
Drizzle in @clarified butter{50%g}(optional).

Continue adding the remaining cream, butter, and clarified butter gradually
until the purée is milky, creamy, and smooth. If it becomes oily or the fat begins
to separate, lower the heat and incorporate a little @warm water{} to restore the
emulsion. This produces =>pommes purée{}.

== step season and serve ==
Season ^pommes purée{} with @kosher salt{}. Transfer it to a warm #serving bowl{}
and finish with @unsalted butter{} and @Maldon salt{}. Serve immediately.
